Massage Soreness: What Is Normal?

Feeling a little different after a massage is common, but understanding what is typical—and what isn't—can help you recover comfortably and know when to seek additional care.

By Brandy Hennigan, LMT • July 2026 • 6-minute read

Many people leave a massage feeling relaxed, lighter, and more comfortable. Others may notice mild soreness that develops later that day or the following morning. While this can be surprising, it is often temporary and may occur after therapeutic bodywork, especially if muscles were already sensitive or tight before the session.

Not everyone experiences soreness, and soreness is not a sign that a massage was "better." Every person's body responds differently.

What Does Normal Soreness Feel Like?

Typical post-massage soreness is often described as feeling similar to the mild muscle soreness that can occur after beginning a new exercise routine. It may feel like tenderness when pressing on an area, stiffness when moving, or a general sense that the muscles have been worked.

For many people, these sensations improve within a day or two without special treatment.

Why Might You Feel Sore?

  • Your muscles were already tense or sensitive before treatment.
  • The massage focused on areas that had not been addressed in a long time.
  • You received deeper or more targeted therapeutic techniques.
  • Your body is adapting to moving differently after the session.
  • You participated in strenuous activity before or shortly after your massage.
A successful massage is measured by how your body feels over time—not by how sore you are afterward.

What Can Help?

If you experience mild soreness, simple self-care is often enough.

  • Continue gentle movement instead of remaining completely still.
  • Take short walks if comfortable.
  • Drink fluids according to your normal hydration needs.
  • Get adequate sleep and allow your body time to recover.
  • Use heat or ice if recommended by your healthcare provider or if you know one works well for you.
  • Avoid unusually strenuous activity if your body feels fatigued.

Should Massage Hurt?

Therapeutic massage may occasionally feel intense, particularly over areas that are already sensitive. However, pain should never feel alarming, overwhelming, or impossible to tolerate.

Your therapist relies on your feedback. If pressure feels too strong, say something immediately. Effective massage is a partnership, and adjustments can be made throughout the session.

When Should You Contact Your Massage Therapist?

If soreness lasts longer than expected, feels unusual, or concerns you, reach out to your massage therapist. They may be able to explain what you are experiencing, suggest modifications for future sessions, or recommend that you follow up with your healthcare provider.

When to Seek Medical Care

Seek medical evaluation if pain becomes severe, continues to worsen, is accompanied by significant swelling, numbness, weakness, fever, increasing redness, or follows a fall or injury. Massage soreness should not prevent you from obtaining appropriate medical care when symptoms are concerning.

Did You Know?

Many clients feel their best 24 to 48 hours after a massage rather than immediately afterward. Your body may continue adjusting after the session has ended.
